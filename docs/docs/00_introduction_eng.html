Introduction

About these notebooks

This series of pages is an exercise carried out to assess the ability to use Earth Embeddings (vector bases) to model complex phenomena related to spatial features. This work is even more ambitious and takes it one step further by using them in an application in the field of the social sciences: crime incidents.

The intent of the book is to be a self-contained data analysis exercise for using and replicating or improving the current models and training. Thus, the referenced repository contains the scripts needed (both Python and Google Earth Engine) to replicate the analysis by following the given instructions and the pages of this book themselves. At the same time, the pages also aim to be self-contained in terms of content, where a minimal background in machine learning models, applied statistics and programming is needed to understand the present pipelines, including a basic description of geospatial concepts and techniques so the logic of the analysis can be followed based on current practice.

Finally, the repository includes data to deploy 2 small web applications to perform an EDA analysis and a demonstration of the predicted street features based on the analysis.

Thus, anyone with a general background can understand both the content as a stakeholder report and the source code to replicate and use the databases. The logic of the notes is as follows:

  • Understand what an embedding is.
  • How embeddings are typically used as features.
  • Data processing, modeling and training decisions.
  • Conclusion.

In addition, there are 3 appendices with the following purposes:

  • Briefly explain the algorithms, the metric evaluation and the mathematics behind the algorithms used.
  • Describe particular methods or logics used in the code that require explanation.
  • Explain the basic geospatial analysis and the operations needed to understand the decisions behind each analysis.

Furthermore, the repository itself contains an FAQ section in its README.md file.

Organization and Notebook Context

In general, the development of this work is carried out within the framework of the Research Internship of the Master’s in Data Science at ITAM, which, as the educational organization that promotes and sponsors this project, in its pursuit of contributing to the vision of a fairer and more prosperous society, opens the way to the open development of solutions and applications of Data Science techniques with the purpose of understanding, disseminating, studying and developing phenomena of a scientific, social, economic and cultural nature. It is in this context that the present work responds to the need to provide a starting point in the use of state-of-the-art tools and data for the understanding of social phenomena such as crime. The problem is of interest to the institution, since the purpose, beyond a model, explanation or final implementation, is to provide tools and a methodology for the development of data-based applications and strategies for organizations and entities interested in modeling the phenomenon of crime, among them:

  • Public Security Institutions
  • Government agencies in general
  • Human rights and public policy observers
  • Researchers in the field
  • Civil Society and
  • The business sector.

In a context where code development solutions seem to be within everyone’s reach and, in parallel, the gap in access to information and to advances in data science development and analysis on the part of civil society appears to be increasingly concentrated in a few institutions and bodies, it is imperative to make public and to set a precedent in closing the gap between leading, frontier models and algorithms and their application to research and to the understanding of phenomena that impact society and communities. It is therefore intended that the present work be a starting point in the conversation toward the development of data-based social research within the ITAM community and, in general, for practitioners and researchers.
